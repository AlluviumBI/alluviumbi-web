Refresh fails. Someone messages the analyst.

A VP needs access. Someone messages the analyst.

“What does this include?” Someone messages the analyst.

The tenant has workspaces, apps, and a gateway appliance. The company still has one human routing path. That person is not a steward. They are infrastructure.

A bus-factor of one is not a compliment. It is an unowned product wearing a hero.

## Occupied is not the same as owned

This is not folklore inside the file: [tribal knowledge in the data model](/blog/tribal-knowledge-in-the-data-model). You can document the model and still route every runtime question to one inbox.

This is not an empty chair: [why your Power BI project has no owner](/blog/power-bi-project-has-no-owner). That chair is empty. This one is full. The analyst became the path around the empty seats.

This is not [who may change a measure](/blog/who-can-change-a-measure). Change control is the formula. The gateway-person also resets credentials, grants access, explains the pack, and reruns Thursday’s refresh.

Waiting on IT is a different queue: [the hidden cost of waiting on IT](/blog/hidden-cost-waiting-on-it-for-reports). Here the queue is one named human who absorbed operations, support, and meaning. The appliance can have a backup: [when refresh fails before stand-up](/blog/gateway-refresh-7am-surprise). This post is the person every question has to cross.

Hero culture feels efficient until they are out. Then you discover you had a bridge, not a program.

## What “the gateway” actually does

They keep the refresh jobs in their head. Access as a favor. The sentence for what Revenue includes. The meeting, because someone has to drive the page. Delivery never split runtime, access, and meaning, so one competent person absorbed all three.

## The costs of a person-as-gateway

1. **Vacation is an outage.** Not a courtesy delay. Refresh, access, and “what does this include” pause. Close week does not pause. The company will still call it a staffing issue. It is an architecture issue with a calendar.

2. **The queue is a person, not a backlog.** Requests die in chat. There is no intake, no “we are done,” no steward list. The loudest DM wins. Standing questions stay heroic extracts because the hero is available: [executive questions that should never be ad-hoc](/blog/questions-that-should-never-be-adhoc).

3. **Definitions live in messages.** The measure description is empty because the analyst will explain it live. When they are not in the thread, the room invents a sentence. Trust becomes whoever spoke last.

4. **Access is a favor, not a control.** Personal shares bypass groups. Row-level security gets waived “just this once.” When the analyst leaves, nobody can say who can see the number—or who should.

5. **Refresh is tribal operations.** The job succeeds most days. The runbook is a person. Capacity collisions, credential rotation, and close-week order live in one head. Finance learns about staleness from a VP, not from an on-call.

6. **Quality drops because one seat does three jobs.** The same person builds, supports, and explains. Build loses. Support is endless. Meaning stays oral. You cannot hire a successor because the posting would say “owns Power BI,” which is not a role. It is a confession.

7. **Hero culture hides underfunding.** Leadership praises responsiveness. That praise postpones a steward, a backup, a group-based access path, and a refresh SLA. The compliment is the cover.

8. **The product cannot be inherited.** A successor can be walked through the file and still fail on Monday, because Monday is not the file. Monday is the routing: who to call, which workspace is a lie, which refresh to rerun first. People do not scale. Paths do.

A bus-factor of one is a close risk, an access risk, and a meaning risk wearing the same hoodie.

## How to stop routing the company through one inbox

1. **Split runtime, access, and meaning.** Refresh and gateway operations get an on-call and a runbook. Access gets groups and a request path. Meaning gets a named steward who can say the sentence. One human may still do two of these in a small shop. They should not be the only path for all three.

2. **Write the three things the DMs currently hold.** As-of and refresh order. Who is in which group. The measure sentences the room actually asks. If it only exists in chat, it is not a product.

3. **Put refresh on an SLA with a backup.** Success is the certified model current through the agreed grain before the first meeting that uses it. A second person must be able to rerun, rotate, and declare stale. If that sentence is impossible, you have a gateway-person, not a gateway.

4. **Move access off personal shares.** Apps, groups, roles. The analyst should not be a permissioning desk. Exceptions get a named expiry. [Row-level security is who sees the number](/blog/row-level-security-who-sees-the-number)—not who the analyst likes this week.

5. **Name a steward who is not the only builder.** The steward owns the sentence and the change path. The builder ships. If they are the same person, at least name a backup steward and a backup builder. Occupied chairs still need deputies.

6. **Publish “what does this include” where the page lives.** Description, glossary, or a certified info page. The next “is freight in this” should not require a human to be online. If the model cannot say it, that is a product gap.

7. **Cross-train before the next outage.** Not a slide hour. A second person runs close-week refresh, grants a group, and explains Revenue out loud. If they cannot, the bus-factor is still one. You just scheduled it.

8. **Measure the inbox.** How many requests are refresh, access, or meaning. That mix is your org chart. A pile of meaning questions is a steward problem. A pile of access questions is an identity problem. A pile of refresh questions is an operations problem. Stop hiring “another analyst” as the universal solvent.

9. **Stop praising the bottleneck.** Thank the person. Fund the path. A compliment without a deputy is how you lose them and the estate in the same quarter.

## What a company with a path looks like

Refresh has an SLA and a named backup. Access is a group. The room can read what a measure includes without waiting. The analyst still builds. They are no longer the appliance. Vacation is inconvenient. It is not an outage.

## Executive takeaway

If refresh, access, and meaning all route through one person, you do not have a Power BI program. You have a bus-factor.

That is not a compliment. Split the path. Write the runbook. Name a backup. Put the sentence on the product.
