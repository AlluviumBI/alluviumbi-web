Staffing a Power BI program is a make-or-buy call. Hire for ownership. Contract for the first trusted loop if you do not have one.

In-house is enough when someone can freeze a definition, cut scope, and keep a meeting on one number. Outside help is enough when you need that loop built once, taught, and left behind. Help becomes a trap when you rent a hero forever and never name an owner.

![Black-and-white two pairs of work gloves hanging on a split-rail fence](/blog/when-to-bring-in-help-hero.jpg)

## This is not a rate card, and it is not “IT versus the business”

There are no day rates or savings claims here, because the bill is local.

IT can host and finance can consume, but neither automatically owns the product ([why your Power BI project has no owner](/blog/power-bi-project-has-no-owner)). A partner does not fix that. Without an accountable internal seat, a partner is just another interested party.

This is also not about buying another platform. [Don’t buy another tool until one model works](/blog/dont-buy-another-tool-until-one-model-works), and the same rule applies to people. Do not buy a bench to avoid finishing a loop.

If you cannot name who will own the model after the contract ends, you are not buying delivery. You are renting a person-shaped gap.

## When in-house is enough

**You have a steward who can say no.** Grain and measures stay frozen, and VPs do not get a silent fork. That seat can be a controller, a plant leader, or a sales-ops lead. It cannot be “the BI team” acting as a committee.

**You have a modeler who can ship a product, not a file.** That means relationships, refresh, and descriptions a successor can read. If the filter lives in one head, you do not have capacity. You have a single point of failure: [tribal knowledge in the data model](/blog/tribal-knowledge-in-the-data-model).

**You have a meeting that will change.** The pack will point at the app, and the twin will retire. [Change is not a poster](/blog/change-management-for-analytics).

**The first loop already exists.** It may be ugly and cover one domain. If Monday already runs on a certified number, the next work is expansion and hygiene, not rescue. Hire or grow people to own it, and keep advisory light.

If those four are true, a partner is optional. Use one for a spike such as a gateway problem, row-level security, or a retirement wave. Do not park them in every stand-up.

## When to bring in help

Bring someone in when the missing piece is the first trusted loop and you cannot staff it without stopping the close.

**No one can sequence stakeholders and the first freeze at the same time.** [Programs stall on delivery](/blog/analytics-programs-fail-in-delivery), not on the visual. A borrowed lead for a proof is honest. A year of status meetings is not.

**You have authors and no product.** Pro licenses everywhere, five revenues, no steward. Hiring into that shape multiplies the copies. You need a cut and a model first, then you hire to *keep* it.

**The meeting will not move without an outside no.** Internal politics can make every domain equally urgent. A partner paid to pick one decision in thirty days is cheaper than a polite stall: [proof of value in 30 days](/blog/proof-of-value-in-30-days).

**You need a ritual you have never run.** First certified model, first retirement, first QBR with two named measures. Doing it once with someone who has run the ritual is how the internal owner inherits a map instead of a mystery.

Help does not replace hiring for ongoing ownership. Refresh and measure control are jobs. If you only contract, the model goes tribal again when the contractor leaves.

## What the wrong mix costs

1. **A contractor becomes the system of record.** They leave, the descriptions are empty, and the company cannot inherit the file. You paid for a loop you cannot run.

2. **Hiring ahead of a product creates sprawl.** New analysts publish and nobody certifies, so you staffed a print shop. [Dashboard sprawl is a tax](/blog/dashboard-sprawl-is-a-tax), and headcount just made it grow faster.

3. **A partner without a cut becomes a backlog factory.** More pages, same unowned measures. Steering likes the activity while the meeting still runs on email.

4. **Internal pride delays the first number.** “We should own this” is right as a destination. It is wrong as a reason to spend another quarter with no signed measure. Ownership starts once there is something to own.

5. **Two benches share one definition gap.** Internal and external teams both build, and neither may freeze grain, so you bought conflict. [Single source of truth is a decision](/blog/single-source-of-truth-is-a-decision), not a staffing plan.

6. **Advisory never turns into a handoff.** A retainer that does not name the internal successor is just staffing. Ask who signs the measure in six months. If the answer is the vendor, you did not buy help. You outsourced the steward.

If the chair still cannot say who owns the number, the mix failed.

## How to split the work

1. **Name the three seats first.** The steward freezes definitions, operations owns refresh, and the consumer leader owns the meeting. If you cannot staff the steward and consumer seats internally, do not start a “program.” Start a smaller loop and tell the truth. Hosting stays with IT. Definitions do not.

2. **Use outside help for the first loop, not the forever queue.** Spend thirty days or a quarter on one domain and one meeting. Teach the map, write the sentences, sit in the room, and then stop building pages. A [Quickstart](/power-bi-quickstart) is this shape. The [90-day program](/blog/first-90-days-of-a-power-bi-program) is what you run after the proof, with the steward named.

3. **Hire to keep the product.** You need a model owner, a refresh owner, and someone who can [change a measure under control](/blog/who-can-change-a-measure). Job descriptions should say product, not “report factory.”

4. **Keep partners on spikes and reviews.** Good uses are retirement waves, RLS, a nasty source, or a quarterly challenge of the certified list. [Managed advisory](/managed-advisory-retainer) helps as a conscience and a backup, not a shadow staff. [Change leadership](/data-project-management-change-leadership) helps when the twin will not die.

5. **Write the handoff as a deliverable.** It covers descriptions, the date table, measure sentences, the refresh runbook, and who to call when the 7 a.m. job fails. If it is not written down, you rented a hero.

6. **Say no to help that needs a new stack to start.** If the first meeting requires a platform program, you are buying a slide. Prove one model on what you already license. A named decision still belongs in a [roadmap](/analytics-ai-strategy-roadmap), not as a reason to staff both benches.

In-house is enough more often than a sales pitch admits. Help is enough more often than pride admits. The test is the loop.

## What good looks like

A named internal person can freeze a measure without a meeting about meetings. Refresh is a job, not a favor. A partner can leave and the Monday pack still opens, and the next hire is for ownership of a product that already exists.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should we hire a full BI team before we start?**
Not before one trusted loop. A team without a product invents work. Prove the meeting, then hire to keep it.

**Is a partner a failure of internal talent?**
No. A partner for a first ritual is how internal talent inherits a map. A partner as the only steward is the failure.

**When should we stop the contract?**
When the steward can run the loop without the builder in the room, and the runbook is actually used. Calendar end dates without that test just restart the gap.
