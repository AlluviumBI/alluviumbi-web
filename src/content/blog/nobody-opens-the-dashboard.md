The report went live. The meeting still runs on a spreadsheet.

Nobody opens the dashboard. That is not a mystery of “change.” It is usually wrong grain, slow pages, untrusted numbers, or no owner. Training will not fix a report that fails the Tuesday meeting.

![Black-and-white unused dirt path through a forest of ferns](/blog/nobody-opens-the-dashboard-hero.jpg)

## Adoption is usage, not a kickoff

People search “Power BI adoption problems” and “why no one uses the dashboard” after the launch email. Usage is the test. Procurement is a different test. If the issue is seats you bought and never assigned well, that is [unused Power BI licenses](/blog/unused-power-bi-licenses). This post is the report that exists and still loses Tuesday morning.

[Judgment:] a dashboard nobody opens is not a training gap first. It is a product that does not replace the habit.

Habit wins when the old file is faster, trusted, or closer to the decision. Infomaze and other practitioners keep repeating that pattern. Treat it as field judgment, not an Alluvium survey: the new report has to *replace* the manual pack, not sit beside it.

If BI never mapped to a decision, that is strategy: [Why Your Power BI Strategy Isn’t Delivering](/blog/power-bi-strategy-alignment). If the estate is clutter, that is [governance](https://www.alluviumbi.com/blog/the-hidden-costs-of-poor-power-bi-governance-and-how-to-fix-it). Here the symptom is an empty usage log and a full inbox.

## Why they do not open it

Four causes show up together. Separate them or you will train the wrong thing.

**Wrong grain.** Executives get a 4,000-row table. Planners get a vanity KPI with no location. The page does not match the job. People go back to the file that does.

**Slow.** If the page spins, the spreadsheet wins. Nobody waits. Performance is not a nice-to-have. It is whether the report participates in the meeting. Tuning is [dashboard optimization](https://www.alluviumbi.com/power-bi-dashboard-optimization-ai-insights). On the plant floor, lag is also cash and scrap: [slow BI in manufacturing](https://www.alluviumbi.com/blog/slow-bi-costs-manufacturing-downtime). In the ELT room, lag is a closed laptop.

**Untrusted numbers.** One mismatch against the controller’s pack and the dashboard loses for good. Different definitions, sources, or refresh times. See [why reports show different numbers](/blog/why-power-bi-reports-show-different-numbers). Trust is not a color palette.

**No owner.** After go-live, nobody is responsible for the measure, the refresh, or the question “does this still serve the meeting.” The report rots. People notice. They leave.

A fifth cousin: the report never entered the operating cadence. It was demoed. It was not put on the agenda in place of the old slide.

## What unused reports actually cost

Unused does not mean free.

1. **Refresh still runs.** Gateways, capacity, and failure emails cost attention. You pay to keep a museum current.

2. **Meetings still assemble the old pack.** Analysts refresh the workbook the dashboard was meant to kill. You funded two reporting systems. Only one is used.

3. **Trust spreads backward.** “We built Power BI and nobody uses it” becomes a story about the platform, not about one bad page. The next report inherits the shrug.

4. **Shadow files stay official.** The emailed Excel *is* the system of record. Access, versioning, and “which file did the CEO see” stay broken.

5. **Roadmaps get funded on fiction.** Leaders add AI, more pages, more seats, on top of an unused foundation. That is how strategy talks get ahead of Tuesday. Seats without usage are the license article. Usage without a decision is this one.

6. **Good people stop asking.** If the last three reports were noise, the next request never comes. The business quietly leaves analytics.

You do not need a percentage from a vendor blog to act. Open activity. If the meeting owners are not there, the report failed—even if a handful of analysts still click around.

## How to fix adoption (without a training roadshow)

Start with the meeting, not the LMS.

1. **Name the decision and the owner.** Which Tuesday question does this page answer? Who is allowed to change the measure? If you cannot name both, do not train anyone. Retire or rebuild. Roadmap work that is still fuzzy belongs in a [Data & AI Strategy Roadmap](/analytics-ai-strategy-roadmap).

2. **Match grain to the job.** ELT: few measures, stamped as-of, exceptions. Managers: the cut they actually filter. Do not ship one page for every role. Wrong grain is the most common “it’s impressive, I don’t know what it’s telling me.”

3. **Make it faster than the spreadsheet.** If it loses on load time, it loses. Cut visuals. Aggregate. Fix the model. Do not add a training session on patience.

4. **Win the number once.** Tie the page to the same actuals finance will defend. Connect the pack; do not paste. [Excel vs Power BI](/blog/excel-vs-power-bi-financial-reporting) is the actuals split. Adoption dies the first time the two disagree in the room.

5. **Put it on the agenda.** The old slide comes out when the new page is in. Change leadership is not a poster. It is removing the competing file. See [Data Project Management & Change Leadership](https://www.alluviumbi.com/data-project-management-change-leadership). If both remain, the old one wins.

6. **Sunset what nobody opens.** Activity logs are enough. Archive the museum. A smaller app of reports that get used beats a portal of leftovers. Stewardship and sunsetting sit in [governance](https://www.alluviumbi.com/blog/the-hidden-costs-of-poor-power-bi-governance-and-how-to-fix-it)—do the retirement on the pages that already lost Tuesday.

Ongoing ownership of the surviving model is [Managed Data & AI Advisory](/managed-advisory-retainer). Finance packs that never left the scavenger hunt are [finance reporting consulting](/blog/power-bi-for-finance-reporting-consulting).

## What good adoption looks like

The meeting opens the app. The pack and the screen match. Someone drills instead of promising a follow-up file. Refresh failures are treated as meeting risks.

You will still have specialists in Excel. That is fine. They should pull from the same model. They should not be the only people who know the number.

[Judgment:] if you need a campaign to make people open it, the report is not doing a job.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does no one use the Power BI dashboard?**
Usually grain, speed, trust, or ownership—not a lack of slides in onboarding.

**Is this an IT problem or a business problem?**
Both. IT owns refresh and the model. The business owns the decision and the agenda. Adoption fails in the gap.

**Should we train everyone again?**
Not until the page is faster than the spreadsheet and agrees with finance. Training cannot rescue a bad product.

**What if a few power users live in it?**
That is not adoption. That is a specialist tool. Fine for analysts. Not an executive dashboard.

**How is this different from unused licenses?**
Licenses are seats. This is whether the report participates in the work. You can have seats in use on the wrong reports.
