The report went live. The meeting still runs on a spreadsheet.

Nobody opens the dashboard, and that is not some mystery of “change.” The cause is usually wrong grain, slow pages, untrusted numbers, or no owner. Training will not fix a report that fails the Tuesday meeting.

![Black-and-white unused dirt path through a forest of ferns](/blog/nobody-opens-the-dashboard-hero.jpg)

## Adoption is usage, not a kickoff

People search “Power BI adoption problems” and “why no one uses the dashboard” right after the launch email. Usage is the test. Procurement is a different test, and if the issue is seats you bought and never assigned well, read [unused Power BI licenses](/blog/unused-power-bi-licenses). This post is about the report that exists and still loses Tuesday morning.

A dashboard nobody opens is not a training gap first. It is a product that does not replace the habit.

Habit wins when the old file is faster, more trusted, or closer to the decision. Practitioners across the field keep seeing the same pattern. Take it as field judgment, not an Alluvium survey: the new report has to *replace* the manual pack, not sit beside it.

If BI never mapped to a decision, that is a strategy problem, covered in [Why Your Power BI Strategy Isn’t Delivering](/blog/power-bi-strategy-alignment). If the estate is clutter, that is [governance](https://www.alluviumbi.com/blog/the-hidden-costs-of-poor-power-bi-governance-and-how-to-fix-it). Here the symptom is an empty usage log and a full inbox.

## Why they do not open it

Four causes tend to show up together. Separate them, or you will train for the wrong thing.

**Wrong grain.** Executives get a 4,000-row table. Planners get a vanity KPI with no location. The page does not match the job, so people go back to the file that does.

**Slow pages.** If the page spins, the spreadsheet wins, because nobody waits. Performance decides whether the report takes part in the meeting at all. Tuning is what [dashboard optimization](https://www.alluviumbi.com/power-bi-dashboard-optimization-ai-insights) is for. On the plant floor, lag also costs cash and scrap, as covered in [slow BI in manufacturing](https://www.alluviumbi.com/blog/slow-bi-costs-manufacturing-downtime). In the ELT room, lag means a closed laptop.

**Untrusted numbers.** One mismatch against the controller’s pack and the dashboard loses for good. The cause is usually different definitions, sources, or refresh times, explained in [why reports show different numbers](/blog/why-power-bi-reports-show-different-numbers). Trust is not a color palette.

**No owner.** After go-live, nobody is responsible for the measure, the refresh, or the question “does this still serve the meeting?” The report rots, people notice, and they leave.

There is a fifth cousin. The report never entered the operating cadence. It was demoed, but it never replaced the old slide on the agenda.

## What unused reports actually cost

Unused does not mean free.

1. **Refresh still runs.** Gateways, capacity, and failure emails all cost attention. You pay to keep a museum current.

2. **Meetings still assemble the old pack.** Analysts refresh the workbook the dashboard was supposed to kill. You funded two reporting systems and only use one.

3. **Distrust spreads backward.** “We built Power BI and nobody uses it” becomes a story about the platform instead of one bad page. The next report inherits the shrug.

4. **Shadow files stay official.** The emailed Excel file *is* the system of record. Access, versioning, and “which file did the CEO see” stay broken.

5. **Roadmaps get funded on fiction.** Leaders stack AI, more pages, and more seats on top of an unused foundation. That is how strategy talk gets ahead of Tuesday. Seats without usage are the license article. Usage without a decision is this one.

6. **Good people stop asking.** If the last three reports were noise, the next request never comes, and the business quietly walks away from analytics.

You do not need a percentage from a vendor blog to act. Open the activity log. If the meeting owners are not in it, the report failed, even if a handful of analysts still click around.

## How to fix adoption without a training roadshow

Start with the meeting, not the LMS.

1. **Name the decision and the owner.** Which Tuesday question does this page answer, and who is allowed to change the measure? If you cannot name both, do not train anyone. Retire or rebuild. Roadmap work that is still fuzzy belongs in a [Data & AI Strategy Roadmap](/analytics-ai-strategy-roadmap).

2. **Match grain to the job.** The ELT gets a few measures, a stamped as-of, and exceptions. Managers get the cut they actually filter. Do not ship one page for every role. Wrong grain is the most common reason for “it’s impressive, but I don’t know what it’s telling me.”

3. **Make it faster than the spreadsheet.** If it loses on load time, it loses. Cut visuals, aggregate, and fix the model. Do not schedule a training session on patience.

4. **Win the number once.** Tie the page to the same actuals finance will defend, and connect the pack instead of pasting it. [Excel vs Power BI](/blog/excel-vs-power-bi-financial-reporting) explains the actuals split. Adoption dies the first time the two disagree in the room.

5. **Put it on the agenda.** The old slide comes out when the new page goes in. Change leadership is not a poster. It means removing the competing file, as described in [Data Project Management & Change Leadership](https://www.alluviumbi.com/data-project-management-change-leadership). If both stay, the old one wins.

6. **Sunset what nobody opens.** Activity logs are enough evidence. Archive the museum, because a smaller app of reports people use beats a portal of leftovers. Stewardship and sunsetting belong to [governance](https://www.alluviumbi.com/blog/the-hidden-costs-of-poor-power-bi-governance-and-how-to-fix-it), so start the retirement with the pages that already lost Tuesday.

Ongoing ownership of the surviving model is what [Managed Data & AI Advisory](/managed-advisory-retainer) covers. Finance packs that never left the scavenger hunt are the subject of [finance reporting consulting](/blog/power-bi-for-finance-reporting-consulting).

## What good adoption looks like

The meeting opens the app, and the pack matches the screen. Someone drills in instead of promising a follow-up file. Refresh failures get treated as meeting risks.

You will still have Excel specialists, and that is fine. They should pull from the same model, and they should not be the only people who know the number.

If you need a campaign to get people to open it, the report is not doing a job.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does no one use the Power BI dashboard?**
Usually grain, speed, trust, or ownership, not a shortage of onboarding slides.

**Is this an IT problem or a business problem?**
Both. IT owns refresh and the model. The business owns the decision and the agenda. Adoption fails in the gap.

**Should we train everyone again?**
Not until the page is faster than the spreadsheet and agrees with finance. Training cannot rescue a bad product.

**What if a few power users live in it?**
That is not adoption. That is a specialist tool, which is fine for analysts but is not an executive dashboard.

**How is this different from unused licenses?**
Licenses are seats. This is about whether the report takes part in the work. You can have every seat in use on the wrong reports.
