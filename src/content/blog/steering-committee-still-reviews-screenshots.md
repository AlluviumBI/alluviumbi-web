The steering committee pack arrives on Sunday night. Twenty slides. Half of them are screenshots of Power BI pages taken on Friday afternoon.

The app is live. The gateways are green. The committee still manages from pictures.

Screenshots are not a temporary habit. They are the real operating system until someone changes the rules of the room.

![Aerial grayscale of semi-trucks parked in diagonal rows casting long shadows on concrete](/blog/steering-committee-still-reviews-screenshots-hero.jpg)

## Screenshots are a management choice

Teams blame risk aversion, projector anxiety, or “executives who like PowerPoint.” Those explanations are incomplete.

Screenshots win when the meeting needs a fixed artifact, when live filters feel unsafe in public, when commentary has nowhere to live in the app, or when the agenda still assumes a prepared pack.

This is cousin to [ask the model, don’t screenshot](/blog/ask-the-model-dont-screenshot) and to [Power BI is live, management cadence never changed](/blog/power-bi-is-live-management-cadence-never-changed). The technology can be ready. The committee ritual can still refuse it.

A screenshot freezes context. That can be useful. It also freezes errors, outdated filters, and last week’s as-of without anyone noticing until the argument starts.

When the steering committee reviews pictures of a live system, you are paying twice: once for the platform, once for the theater that ignores it.

## Why the pack stays in screenshots

The agenda was never rewritten. Item three still says “review the slides,” not “open the certified app page.”

Leaders fear a live click that reveals a blank drillthrough or a wrong plant filter. Past failures taught caution. Caution became policy.

Commentary does not fit the page. Screenshots get pasted into slides where narrative can sit beside the picture. The model never grew a place for decisions and owners.

Version control feels safer in a deck. “This is what we reviewed on the 12th” is easy with a PDF. Harder when the live page moved overnight—unless you design a governed freeze.

Politics prefers prepared narratives. A live model answers follow-ups. A screenshot supports the story someone already wrote.

## The costs of steering by screenshot

1. **The committee and the model diverge.** By Wednesday the live page moved. The decision was made on Friday’s picture. Reconciliation becomes a standing agenda item.

2. **Accountability attaches to the slide author.** The room asks who built the pack, not who owns the outcome. Management quality drops.

3. **Follow-up questions leave the room.** Screenshots do not drill. The interesting question becomes an offline request. Decision latency returns.

4. **Filter mistakes fossilize.** A wrong slicer in Friday’s capture becomes next week’s “official” number. Silent lies travel farther than live pages that can be corrected in place.

5. **Analytics teams optimize for capture, not decisions.** Pages are designed to look good in a rectangle on a slide. Grain and interaction lose to screenshot aesthetics.

6. **Adoption metrics lie.** Opens look fine. The management system still runs on PowerPoint. Same false green as export-heavy “adoption.”

7. **Security theater appears.** Sensitive pages get pasted into widely forwarded decks. Access controls on the app do not protect the screenshot chain.

8. **You teach leaders the model is optional.** Every successful screenshot meeting trains the organization to skip the product you funded.

## How to fix it: change the committee artifact rules

1. **Rewrite one recurring agenda item.** Pick the operating review or the steering committee. Replace “review slides of KPIs” with “review page X in the app” or “review the governed pack generated from model Y.”

2. **Decide where a freeze is legitimate.** If the committee needs a fixed record, produce a scheduled paginated pack or labeled snapshot from the same semantic model. Do not pretend random screenshots are that freeze.

3. **Put commentary and owners next to the metric.** Minutes, actions, and narrative need a home. If the only home is PowerPoint, screenshots will win forever.

4. **Prove the live path once under real conditions.** Same RLS, same filters, same drill paths the chair will use. Fix blank pages and wrong defaults before mandating live use. See the drillthrough problem as a prerequisite, not a later enhancement.

5. **Ban unofficial captures for decision records.** Allow screenshots for illustration in email. Do not allow them as the system of record for steering decisions. State that in the charter.

6. **Make the default landing safe.** Land on the right company, period, and page without a scavenger hunt. Leaders screenshot when they fear live mis-clicks.

7. **Align the pack with [certified datasets](/blog/certified-datasets-vs-wild-west).** If the committee still pastes from three workspaces, fix the source map first. Screenshots of inconsistent models multiply the definition fight.

8. **Time-box the transition.** Two meetings with dual running. Then the old screenshot pack retires on a date, same discipline as [how to retire a dashboard](/blog/how-to-retire-a-dashboard).

9. **Measure success by decisions, not by fewer JPEGs alone.** Did follow-ups stay in the model? Did owners get assigned in the room? Did the next meeting start from the same numbers?

## What good looks like

The steering committee opens a known app page or a governed pack with a clear as-of. Screenshots may still appear in narrative appendices. They are not the authority.

When someone asks “what changed since last month,” the room clicks—not forwards another email chain of cropped tiles.

The analytics team stops formatting for capture and starts designing for the decision the agenda actually owns.

## Screenshots versus governed packs

There is a legitimate need for a fixed committee record. That need does not require a collage of cropped tiles from Friday afternoon.

A governed pack has a known as-of, a known model, and a known distribution list. A screenshot chain has none of those properties. It only has convenience.

If your committee cannot yet run fully live, invest in the governed pack first. Kill the unofficial captures second. Trying to ban screenshots while offering no freeze guarantees a quiet rebellion back to PowerPoint.

## What the chair must signal

The chair sets the artifact rule. If the chair accepts screenshots as the decision record, no analytics roadmap will win.

Ask for one sentence in the charter: decisions are recorded against the certified app page or the governed pack, not against personal captures. Then enforce it for two cycles. Culture follows the gavel.

## Executive takeaway

If the steering committee still reviews screenshots, Power BI is a content library for PowerPoint—not a management system.

Change the artifact rules. Offer a governed freeze when you need one. Make the live path safe. Then stop paying for a platform the room only visits long enough to take a picture.
