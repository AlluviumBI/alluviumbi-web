A new page for every question is how you get sprawl. It feels like service. It is demand with no intake.

Mid-market leaders get a Slack ping, a hallway ask, a board follow-up. Someone commissions a dashboard. A week later there is another pbix. The original question was one filter on a model you already had—or should have had. You funded a brochure. You underfunded the product.

![Black-and-white forest path splitting into several trails](/blog/stop-commissioning-a-dashboard-per-question-hero.jpg)

## This is intake, not the cleanup of last year’s files

[Dashboard sprawl is a tax](/blog/dashboard-sprawl-is-a-tax) is the estate you already have: inventory, certify, retire. This piece is how the next file gets born. Demand management. Who is allowed to turn a question into a build.

If you only retire, the hose is still on. If you only freeze publishing, questions move to email. You installed a backlog in the inbox.

The search is real: “too many dashboard requests,” “Power BI intake process.” People are not asking for a portal. They are asking how to stop saying yes to a page every time a VP is curious.

[From dashboards to decisions](/blog/from-dashboards-to-decisions) is the strategy test: is this even a decision. This post is the operating rule after that test. Recurring questions hit the model. One-offs do not get a workspace.

## How a question becomes a file

Someone needs a cut. Region. Product line. A week. The certified app does not have that slicer—or nobody looked. A builder copies a report. They publish. The question is answered once.

Next month the question returns with a different week. The copy is stale. Another copy. Now you have three brochures and one unanswered product need: a dimension and a measure with an owner.

Self-service was supposed to be explore-on-the-model. Commissioning a dashboard is explore-by-fork. Forks are how [every team builds their own model](/blog/every-team-built-their-own-model) when the ask is big, and how sprawl returns when the ask is small.

IT feels this as a ticket queue: [the hidden cost of waiting on IT](/blog/hidden-cost-waiting-on-it-for-reports). The wait is real. So is the reflex to skip the queue by standing up a side file. Both are intake failures.

## What a page-per-question policy costs

1. **You pay for answers that cannot be reused.** A one-off page does not accumulate. The next similar question starts at zero. The model would have compounded. The brochure does not.

2. **Builders never get to the product.** Every sprint is a new canvas. Grain never freezes. [The semantic model is the product](/blog/semantic-model-is-the-product). If funding follows pages, the product starves. You will [rebuild the same report every quarter](/blog/rebuilding-the-same-report-every-quarter).

3. **The official app gets quieter.** Leadership cannot find the number because the last answer lived in a side workspace named after a person. Adoption falls. They commission another page. The loop feeds itself.

4. **No one can say no, so nothing is done.** A program without a cut is a queue: [analytics programs fail in delivery](/blog/analytics-programs-fail-in-delivery). Each yes is polite. The certified measure slips a month.

5. **Questions that should be ad-hoc become assets.** A board follow-up is not a product. Treating it like one creates a report that must refresh forever. Capacity groans. Ghosts accumulate. Then you buy [headroom instead of a rule](/blog/premium-capacity-is-not-a-strategy).

6. **Finance inherits twins they will not sign.** Extra pages drift extra filters. The controller sees a total that does not tie. They refuse the whole app. [Sign-off is a feature](/blog/finance-wont-sign-off-on-the-dashboard). Intake that bypasses the steward is how you earn that refusal.

## What not to do

Do not stand up a request portal that still ships a dashboard for every ticket. A form is not a policy. The policy is: model first, page only if the question is recurring and owned.

Do not make executives wait two months to slice a certified model. That is how they commission a rebel file. Self-service on the official model is the release valve. Self-service as a new dataset is the leak.

Do not inventory as a substitute for intake. Cleaning last year’s mess without a “how work enters” rule is a one-time hero project.

Do not measure the BI team on tickets closed. Measure them on certified measures in use, and on requests that were answered *without* a new file.

## How to fund the model and starve the one-off page

1. **Install one intake path.** All asks hit the same backlog. Requester, decision, whether it already exists on the model, whether it is recurring. Shadow commissions are how sprawl returns. If a VP can spin a workspace without that card, you do not have a program. You have a suggestion box with licenses.

2. **Triage in three buckets, out loud.** *Already in the app*—train, bookmark, stop. *Model change*—new grain, new measure, new dimension, with a steward. *Working paper*—Excel or a sandbox connected to the model, no official stamp, no 6 a.m. SLA. Most hallway questions are bucket one or three. They die as pages when you say that.

3. **Answer from the model before you open Desktop.** Can a filter, a field, or a measure on the certified semantic model settle it. If yes, do that. Publish nothing new. [Ask the model](/blog/ask-the-model-dont-screenshot) instead of screenshotting a one-off. If the model cannot answer because grain is wrong, that is a product backlog item, not a canvas.

4. **Require a steward for anything that will refresh twice.** A page that must live needs an owner who can freeze the definition. No steward, no schedule. That rule alone kills vanity dashboards. It is the same ownership split as [a project with no owner](/blog/power-bi-project-has-no-owner).

5. **Time-box one-offs and refuse to promote them by default.** A sandbox report can exist for a board cycle. Then it expires. Promotion to the app is a decision: recurring, signed, tied. [The first ninety days](/blog/first-90-days-of-a-power-bi-program) should prove this loop in one domain before you scale intake theater.

6. **Review demand like a PMO, not like a help desk.** Rank against the P&L. Cut. Publish the no’s. Change leadership is part of the work: [data project management](https://www.alluviumbi.com/data-project-management-change-leadership). If the backlog is only pages, rewrite it as products. Strategy without a cut needs a [roadmap](https://www.alluviumbi.com/-data-ai-strategy-roadmap) that names decisions, not a longer wish list.

Start where the hose is worst. One VP. One standing meeting. Prove that questions can die as filters.

## What good looks like

The ELT still asks sharp questions. They get answers from the app the same day, or they get a dated working paper, or they get a date for a model change. They do not get a new workspace by default.

Builders spend more time on grain than on themes.

The certified set stays short. Sandboxes are labeled. Expiration is real.

When sprawl creeps back, you have a retire list *and* an intake rule. Cleanup without demand management is a season. Intake is the climate.

[Judgment:] if your backlog is a list of dashboards, you are taking orders. If it is a list of model changes and refusals, you are running a program.

## Frequently asked questions

**Won’t this slow us down?**
It slows the first yes. It speeds the tenth question.

**What do we do with true one-offs?**
Answer them. In Excel connected to the model, or in a sandbox with an expiry. Do not put them on the 6 a.m. refresh that the close depends on.

**How is this different from dashboard sprawl?**
Sprawl is inventory you already have. This is the tap. Retire without intake and you will write that post again next year.
