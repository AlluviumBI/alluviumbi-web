Monday needs cash, collections, and exceptions. Not a gallery of unused pages.

A CFO does not start the week in a theme park of visuals. They start with whether the week can be funded, who has not paid, and what broke over the weekend. If the Power BI app opens to twelve pages of last quarter’s story, the CFO will go back to the spreadsheet that already answers those three.

![Black-and-white still harbor at dawn with a low sun on the water](/blog/cash-not-charts-cfo-monday-hero.jpg)

## An information diet, not a dashboard tour

Executive packs fail in two directions. Too little: cash is in a file the controller emails on Wednesday. Too much: every KPI the program ever certified, arranged as a gallery. Neither is a Monday surface.

This is not month-end drag. Close has its own calendar: [why month-end still takes a week](/blog/why-month-end-still-takes-a-week). Monday is the operating week: cash, collections, exceptions that need a call today. Inventory as cash is a cousin—[inventory is cash](/blog/inventory-is-cash-slow-stock-reporting)—if stock traps working capital. It does not belong as page nine of a museum.

Nobody opens a dashboard that is not a job: [nobody opens the dashboard](/blog/nobody-opens-the-dashboard). For a CFO, Monday’s job is narrow. Sprawl is a tax: [dashboard sprawl is not self-service](/blog/dashboard-sprawl-is-a-tax). The exec app should be your most ruthless retire list. Bank, AR, AP, and forecast already exist. They do not meet the CFO in one place, on time, with exceptions on top.

## What Monday actually is

**Cash.** Position you will defend if someone asks this afternoon. Not a thirteen-month art project. Not a waterfall that needs a narrator. In, out, balance, horizon the CFO already uses—week, two weeks, month. Same definitions as treasury, not a BI remix.

**Collections.** What is past terms, who is large, what moved since last Monday. Not a CRM theme page. Not every invoice. The list that would change who gets called.

**Exceptions.** Refresh failed. A plant did not land. A customer bounced. A payment batch stuck. A covenant headroom check the CFO already tracks, if you have one—no theater if you do not. The point is surprises, not a scorecard of green.

Anything else is later in the week, month-end, or someone else’s meeting. Margin belongs in named measures: [margin definitions that don’t survive a meeting](/blog/margin-definitions-that-dont-survive)—not the Monday opener. Board packs late because the *data* was late are a different clock: [your board pack is late because the data isn’t](/blog/board-pack-late-data-isnt). Monday still needs a refresh that ran. If it did not, that is the exception.

## The costs of a gallery

1. **The CFO never lands.** Twelve pages train a skip. They open Excel, the bank portal, and an AR export. You paid for a platform that lost the first five minutes of the week—the minutes that set the agenda.

2. **Collections hide in a tab nobody reaches.** Past-due sits behind a revenue story. The call list is still a file. Cash does not improve because the insight was two clicks further than the habit.

3. **Exceptions arrive as people, not as a list.** A controller texts. A plant emails. Treasury IM’s. The “dashboard” is calm. The week is not. You built a museum of normal and left the abnormal in chat.

4. **Refresh failure is discovered in the meeting.** Monday’s surface should scream if actuals are stale. A gallery of last good refresh looks fine. It is a close risk: [refresh failures are a close risk](/blog/refresh-failures-are-a-close-risk). For cash, stale is not an IT ticket. It is a decision on bad funds.

5. **Working capital decisions lag a week.** Inventory, terms, and payables moves wait for a pack that is built for narrative. The COO already feels fill rate. The CFO feels cash late. Same stock. Different diet.

6. **License and build effort go to pages that cannot survive a Monday.** Usage metrics will show the gallery is empty. The program will propose more training. The diet was wrong. Unused seats often start here: [unused Power BI licenses](/blog/unused-power-bi-licenses).

If the CFO’s first move is still three systems and a paste, the app is not a Monday product.

## What not to do

Do not add a “CFO overview” that restates the gallery in a smaller font.

Do not lead with P&L story on Monday unless that is genuinely how this company funds the week. Most mid-market weeks are cash and collections first.

Do not build a separate “executive theme” with numbers that do not match treasury. Pretty and wrong is worse than ugly and agreed.

Do not hide the as-of. If Monday’s cash is Friday night, say Friday night.

## How to fix it

1. **Write the three questions on a card.** What is our cash position for the horizon we actually manage. Who do we need to collect from now. What broke. If a visual does not answer one of those, it is not on page one. It may not be in the exec app.

2. **One page. Then a list. Then stop.** Position and horizon. Collections table with owner. Exception list with source (refresh, bank, AR, plant). That is the Monday surface. Drill exists. The gallery does not.

3. **Same definitions as treasury and the controller.** Cash is not a new measure invented for a visual. AR aging matches the collection team’s working list. If they disagree, you do not have a CFO page. You have a third dictionary. Fix the sentence in the model first.

4. **Put exceptions above the story.** Failed refresh, missing entity, out-of-balance to bank, a customer that crossed a threshold you already use. Green tiles that hide a red process are how Monday goes quiet and Wednesday goes loud.

5. **Time-box the rest of the week.** Mid-week can hold margin, forecast, and the plant pack. Month-end holds the board story. Do not drag those onto Monday “so they have everything.” Everything is how they have nothing. Retire pages that cannot name the meeting they serve.

6. **Make this the default app.** The CFO should not hunt workspaces. Bookmark the Monday page. The first-week change is the same as any other stop: stop emailing the three files. ## What good looks like

The CFO opens one app, sees as-of, cash, the call list, and whether anything failed. The rest of the week has other rooms.

Treasury does not keep a side file “because the dashboard is for leadership.” Leadership is looking at treasury’s number.

Exceptions are a list with owners, not a thread.

When a page has no job, it leaves the exec app. The diet stays short on purpose.

## FAQ

**What about forecast and covenant packs?** They can live in the same app on another page, used on their own cadence. They are not the Monday opener unless that is truly the first question. Most weeks, it isn’t.

**Do we need real-time bank feeds?** You need a refresh that beats the first decision of the day, with a visible as-of. Intra-day theater is optional. Stale-and-pretty is not.
