A CFO does not start the week in a theme park of visuals. They start with three questions: can the week be funded, who has not paid, and what broke over the weekend. If the Power BI app opens to twelve pages of last quarter’s story, the CFO goes back to the spreadsheet that already answers those three.

![Black-and-white still harbor at dawn with a low sun on the water](/blog/cash-not-charts-cfo-monday-hero.jpg)

## An information diet, not a dashboard tour

Executive packs fail in two directions. With too little, cash lives in a file the controller emails on Wednesday. With too much, every KPI the program ever certified sits in a gallery. Neither one works on a Monday.

Close runs on its own calendar, covered in [why month-end still takes a week](/blog/why-month-end-still-takes-a-week). Monday is the operating week: cash, collections, and exceptions that need a call today. If stock traps working capital, [inventory is cash](/blog/inventory-is-cash-slow-stock-reporting) is a close cousin, but it does not belong as page nine of a museum.

[Nobody opens a dashboard](/blog/nobody-opens-the-dashboard) that does not do a job, and a CFO’s Monday job is narrow. [Dashboard sprawl is a tax](/blog/dashboard-sprawl-is-a-tax), so the executive app should carry your most ruthless retire list. Bank, AR, AP, and forecast data already exist. They just never meet the CFO in one place, on time, with exceptions on top.

## What Monday actually is

**Cash.** This is the position you would defend if someone asked this afternoon. Skip the thirteen-month art project and the waterfall that needs a narrator. Show in, out, balance, and the horizon the CFO already uses (week, two weeks, month), with the same definitions as treasury rather than a BI remix.

**Collections.** Show what is past terms, which accounts are large, and what moved since last Monday. This is the list that changes who gets called, not a CRM theme page or every invoice.

**Exceptions.** A refresh failed, a plant did not land, a customer bounced, or a payment batch got stuck. Include a covenant headroom check if the CFO already tracks one, and leave it out if not. The point is surprises, not a scorecard of green.

Everything else belongs later in the week, at month-end, or in someone else’s meeting. Margin belongs in named measures, as in [margin definitions that don’t survive a meeting](/blog/margin-definitions-that-dont-survive), not in the Monday opener. A board pack that runs late runs on a different clock, covered in [your board pack is late because the data isn’t](/blog/board-pack-late-data-isnt). Monday still needs a refresh that ran. If it did not, that is the first exception.

## The costs of a gallery

1. **The CFO never lands.** Twelve pages teach people to skip. The CFO opens Excel, the bank portal, and an AR export. You paid for a platform that lost the first five minutes of the week, which are the minutes that set the agenda.

2. **Collections hide in a tab nobody reaches.** Past-due sits behind a revenue story and the call list is still a file. Cash does not improve, because the insight was two clicks past the habit.

3. **Exceptions arrive as people, not as a list.** A controller texts, a plant emails, and treasury sends an IM. The “dashboard” is calm while the week is not. You built a museum of normal and left the abnormal in chat.

4. **Refresh failure gets discovered in the meeting.** Monday’s surface should shout if actuals are stale, but a gallery showing the last good refresh looks fine. [Refresh failures are a close risk](/blog/refresh-failures-are-a-close-risk). For cash, stale data is not an IT ticket. It is a decision made on bad funds.

5. **Working capital decisions lag a week.** Inventory, terms, and payables moves wait for a pack built for narrative. The COO feels fill rate right away and the CFO feels cash late. It is the same stock on a different diet.

6. **License and build effort go to pages that cannot survive a Monday.** Usage metrics show the gallery is empty, and the program proposes more training when the diet was the problem. [Unused Power BI licenses](/blog/unused-power-bi-licenses) often start here.

## What not to do

Do not add a “CFO overview” that restates the gallery in a smaller font.

Do not lead with the P&L story on Monday unless that is genuinely how this company funds the week. Most mid-market weeks start with cash and collections.

Do not build a separate “executive theme” with numbers that do not match treasury. Pretty and wrong is worse than ugly and agreed.

Do not hide the as-of. If Monday’s cash is Friday night, say Friday night.

## How to fix it

1. **Write the three questions on a card.** What is our cash position for the horizon we actually manage? Who do we need to collect from now? What broke? If a visual does not answer one of those, it is not on page one, and it may not belong in the executive app at all.

2. **Build one page and one list, then stop.** Show position and horizon, a collections table with owners, and an exception list with its source (refresh, bank, AR, plant). That is the Monday surface. Drill-through can exist. The gallery cannot.

3. **Use the same definitions as treasury and the controller.** Cash is not a new measure invented for a visual, and AR aging should match the collection team’s working list. If they disagree, you do not have a CFO page. You have a third dictionary, so fix the sentence in the model first.

4. **Put exceptions above the story.** Failed refresh, missing entity, out of balance to bank, or a customer that crossed a threshold you already use. Green tiles that hide a red process are how Monday goes quiet and Wednesday goes loud.

5. **Time-box the rest of the week.** Mid-week can hold margin, forecast, and the plant pack, and month-end holds the board story. Do not drag those onto Monday “so they have everything,” because everything is how they end up with nothing. Retire pages that cannot name the meeting they serve.

6. **Make this the default app.** Bookmark the Monday page so the CFO does not hunt through workspaces, and stop emailing the three files. ## What good looks like

The CFO opens one app and sees the as-of, cash, the call list, and whether anything failed. The rest of the week happens in other rooms.

Treasury does not keep a side file “because the dashboard is for leadership,” because leadership is looking at treasury’s number. Exceptions are a list with owners, not a thread. When a page has no job, it leaves the executive app, and the diet stays short on purpose.

## FAQ

**What about forecast and covenant packs?** They can live in the same app on another page, used on their own cadence. They are not the Monday opener unless that is truly the first question, and most weeks it isn’t.

**Do we need real-time bank feeds?** You need a refresh that beats the first decision of the day, with a visible as-of. Intra-day theater is optional. Stale and pretty is not.
