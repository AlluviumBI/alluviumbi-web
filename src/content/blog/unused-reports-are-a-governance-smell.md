Zero views is a signal. Refreshing ghosts is how capacity and trust both leak.

One quiet page can be a product miss. An estate of quiet pages is a governance miss. The usage log is telling you the certified layer never happened, the attic never got emptied, and refresh still treats everything as live.

![Black-and-white unused wooden beehive boxes standing in a field](/blog/unused-reports-are-a-governance-smell-hero.jpg)

## This is the estate, not the empty Tuesday, not the funeral

[Nobody opens the dashboard](/blog/nobody-opens-the-dashboard) is about one report that lost the meeting because it was wrong in grain, slow, untrusted, or ownerless. Fix that page or kill it.

[How to retire a dashboard](/blog/how-to-retire-a-dashboard) is the ritual for one file: owner, replacement, date, announcement. You will use that ritual. This piece is about noticing the *smell* across the tenant, and the cleanup list that goes beyond a one-off funeral.

[Dashboard sprawl is a tax](/blog/dashboard-sprawl-is-a-tax) is the inventory slogan. This post is the signal that slogan missed: usage near zero, refresh still on, and endorsement still looking official. Ghosts with a schedule.

If you cannot say which reports the company actually runs, you do not have governance. You have hosting.

## What the smell actually is

Usage is not a vanity metric. It shows which products take part in decisions.

A handful of analyst clicks is not a meeting. A report that only the author opens is a working paper that escaped. A certified badge on a file nobody has opened in a quarter is a lying label.

Ghosts cost money even when seats are assigned. [Unused licenses](/blog/unused-power-bi-licenses) covers the invoice side, and this covers the workspace side. You can have full seats and still refresh a museum.

The smell shows up as a cluster:

**Refresh of the unviewed.** Gateways, capacity, and 6 a.m. collisions eat the morning window with jobs nobody needed, as in [the 7 a.m. surprise](/blog/gateway-refresh-7am-surprise).

**Endorsement without an audience.** Promoted and certified only mean “safe” if people can find the live one. A wall of badges is camouflage.

**Twins of the live pack.** The certified app is quiet because the old name still works. Adoption failed at the front door, estate-wide.

**No owner on the long tail.** Authors left and bookmarks remain. Access that was fine for a project is not fine two years later. See [hidden costs of Power BI governance](/blog/the-hidden-costs-of-poor-power-bi-governance-and-how-to-fix-it).

Pull the usage you have and ask the EA who builds the pack. If both are quiet on a file, that is data. It is not a reason to keep the schedule “just in case.”

## What an unviewed estate costs

1. **Capacity leaks on ghosts.** Unused reports still refresh, still fail, and still page someone at dawn. You buy headroom to feed the attic. [Premium capacity is not a strategy](/blog/premium-capacity-is-not-a-strategy), and turning jobs off is the cheaper capacity plan.

2. **Trust leaks from the label.** If “certified” includes files nobody uses, the word means nothing. People keep the emailed workbook, and the next certified page inherits the shrug.

3. **Risk sits where nobody looks.** Old RLS, stale vendor lists, and columns that should have died with a project all linger. Review follows usage, so unviewed means unreviewed.

4. **The live product stays buried.** Navigation becomes a scavenger hunt, and the steward’s app looks empty because thirty other doors still open. Sprawl was the tax. Ghosts are how the tax compounds after the build.

5. **Analysts rebuild instead of pointing.** Copying feels safer than trusting a quiet catalog, so drift continues and you pay to explain two truths: [why reports show different numbers](/blog/why-power-bi-reports-show-different-numbers).

6. **Roadmaps get funded on fiction.** Leaders add pages and tools on top of an unused foundation. The estate looks busy in a workspace list, but the meetings never moved.

Open the activity data. If the meeting owners are not there, the file failed, even if a handful of clicks still land.

## The cleanup list (estate level)

Do this as one pass, then keep it as hygiene. Individual funerals still use the ritual. The pass is how you build the queue.

1. **Inventory with usage, not with pride.** For each report, list the workspace, last view by a consumer (not the author), refresh on or off, endorsement, and named owner. No owner plus no views is the first pile. Author-only views is the second. Real usage with no owner is a rescue, not a funeral.

2. **Split the piles in writing.** *Ghosts* have near-zero consumer use for a cycle with refresh still on. *Twins* have usage but duplicate a certified decision. *Live* reports are the ones the meeting actually opens. Only live reports stay in the app people are told to use. Ghosts and twins get dates.

3. **Turn refresh off before you argue taste.** For ghosts, the first win is the morning window. A dark report that still refreshes is the smell, so disable the schedule. Keep the file visible with a banner if you must. Capacity comes back before the political fight starts.

4. **Strip lying labels.** Remove certified and promoted from anything that is not live and owned. Endorsement is a promise, and an unused report is a broken one. Recertify only the thin layer, as in [certified models vs the wild west](/blog/certified-datasets-vs-wild-west).

5. **Queue funerals instead of declaring a mass grave.** Each ghost still needs an owner, a replacement or a “decision dead” note, and a date, following the ritual post. Batch announcements by domain. Do not delete a tenant on a Friday, and do not recertify everything as a substitute for retirement.

6. **Close the hose.** Cleanup without intake is a season. Stop commissioning a page per question, and put standing executive asks in the model, as in [questions that should never be ad-hoc](/blog/questions-that-should-never-be-adhoc). Review usage monthly like incidents. A copy with a new name is a resurrection, so send it through commissioning, not nostalgia.

Change leadership is how the live door becomes the default, as in [data project management](/data-project-management-change-leadership). If nobody can say which decisions the estate serves, you need a [roadmap](/analytics-ai-strategy-roadmap) before a purge. A [Quickstart](/power-bi-quickstart) can be the one live loop you point people to while the attic goes dark. Ongoing conscience is [managed advisory](/managed-advisory-retainer).

## What good looks like

You can name the reports the ELT actually runs, and it is a short list. Refresh jobs match that list plus a labeled sandbox with no endorsement.

Certified means used and owned. Unused means dark, then gone, and the attic does not refill every quarter.

## Frequently asked questions

**What if something unused is needed once a year?**
Then it is not a daily product. Label it and refresh it on a calendar that matches the use, or run it as a working paper with an owner. Do not keep a daily job for an annual question.

**Should we delete everything with zero views this month?**
No. Prove neglect or replacement, then use the ritual. Silent takedowns are how zombies come back by Monday.

**Is low usage always a failure?**
For an executive pack, yes. For a steward’s diagnostic page, maybe not. The smell is an *estate* of unviewed, unowned, still-refreshing files wearing a badge. One quiet diagnostic is not the signal.
