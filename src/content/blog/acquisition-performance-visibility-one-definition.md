Private equity does not ask for another dashboard. It asks for the same margin language across every add-on.

Five plants still open five Excels. Each workbook has a revenue column, and none of them mean the same thing at the same grain. The first board pack turns into a reconciliation exercise wearing a Power BI skin.

Acquisition performance visibility starts with one definition that is named, owned, and shared before anyone debates chart color.

![Industrial worker grinding a metal beam with a bright spray of sparks](/blog/acquisition-performance-visibility-one-definition-hero.svg)

## Same language, or the roll-up is theater

Mid-market manufacturing portfolios live and die on comparable plant performance: contribution margin, working capital, scrap, on-time. The labels sound universal. The logic rarely is.

Plant A books freight in COGS. Plant B parks it below the line. Plant C adjusts for intercompany the week after flash. Combine those in a matrix and you get a precise-looking lie.

[The PE firm just closed and the reporting bar moved](/blog/pe-firm-closed-reporting-bar-moved). Under that bar, visibility means finance will defend the same measure in every plant review. It does not mean every plant gets a personal report.

If [every team built their own model](/blog/every-team-built-their-own-model), the portfolio roll-up is five truths competing for one slide. Standardization is how you end the competition.

## Where "one definition" fails in practice

Teams start with visuals because demos reward charts and definitions feel like homework.

Grain stays implicit. "Margin by plant" never says whether plant means shipping location, producing location, or P&L owner. Two slicers later, the totals diverge.

Measures grow by ticket. "Match Plant B's Excel" produces a private CALCULATE, and the certified model quietly ends up hosting three margins.

Working-capital metrics arrive last because they are hard. Inventory timing, AR aging, and AP cutoffs disagree across ERPs, so the pack ships without them and the cash conversation stays offline.

Nobody is named as the person who may change Revenue. When [measures nobody can explain](/blog/measures-nobody-can-explain) sit behind a board tile, trust is borrowed from the author instead of earned by the product.

Add-ons keep local Excel as the "real" close, and Power BI becomes a mirror leaders ignore when the number matters.

## The costs of dashboard-first visibility

1. **The board pack fails the first hard question.** "Why did Plant C margin move?" Nobody can walk through the inclusions without opening three files.

2. **Finance will not sign what it cannot defend.** Controllers hold back their endorsement and ops fills the gap with spreadsheets. It is the same pattern as [finance won't sign off on the dashboard](/blog/finance-wont-sign-off-on-the-dashboard).

3. **Plant comparisons turn political.** Managers argue methodology instead of scrap or price, and operating reviews stall.

4. **Working capital stays a month-end surprise.** Inventory and AR only reconcile after the combine, so cash actions start late.

5. **Self-service multiplies definitions.** Analysts publish lookalike measures in personal workspaces. [Self-service without five revenues](/blog/self-service-without-five-revenues) is the bar. Miss it and sprawl speeds up under a PE logo.

6. **Add-on integration repeats the mess.** Each new plant invents local KPIs. The portfolio thesis assumed synergy, and reporting delivers fragmentation.

7. **Tool spend looks like success while decisions stay slow.** License counts rise. Monday still waits on Excel.

8. **Trust debt compounds every quarter.** One wrong roll-up teaches leaders to discount the app for good.

## How to fix it: definition before decoration

1. **Write the business sentence for each KPI.** State what contribution margin includes and excludes, at what grain, and on what calendar. If the sentence is fuzzy, do not build the visual.

2. **Lock shared grain across add-ons.** Item, plant, customer, and period follow the portfolio standard first. Local attributes can extend the keys but cannot redefine them.

3. **Publish a short measure dictionary in the model.** Names, descriptions, and owners travel with the semantic model, not in a wiki nobody opens.

4. **Collapse duplicate margins into one owned measure.** Find every Gross Margin, CM, and "Adj Margin." Keep one. Label the rest unofficial or delete them.

5. **Bring the same discipline to working capital.** Inventory, AR, and AP need source-of-truth rules and timing. Do not leave cash metrics in "phase two forever."

6. **Treat the semantic model as the product.** Reports are skins. [The semantic model is the product](/blog/semantic-model-is-the-product). Portfolio apps connect to certified measures and do not fork the logic.

7. **Require a finance and ops co-sign before promotion.** A second reader must be able to explain the measure out loud. If they cannot, it is not board-ready.

8. **Separate plant exploration from certified portfolio measures.** Plants can explore. The operating pack cannot host experiments.

9. **Put definition drift on the monthly checklist.** After close, scan for new private measures and workbook twins. Drift is how standardization dies quietly.

10. **Onboard add-ons through the dictionary.** New plants map their sources into existing measures instead of inventing a parallel language.

## What good looks like

An operating partner asks for margin by plant and one measure answers. Plant managers recognize the number from their floor reviews. Finance can walk the inclusions in under a minute without the original author in the room.

Working-capital tiles use the same period logic as the P&L, so cash and margin conversations share a calendar.

New acquisitions inherit the dictionary on day one of integration planning, not as a cleanup twelve months later.

## A practical standardization sprint

Week one: pick five portfolio KPIs behind the board pack. Draft one business sentence for each and mark where plants disagree today.

Week two: settle the disagreements in a working session with finance and ops, not in DAX. Document the winners.

Week three: build the five measures in one slice of the semantic model. Kill or quarantine the duplicates.

Week four: run one real operating review from those measures only, and ban side Excels for those five questions. Capture the exceptions. Fix definitions, not chart polish.

Then repeat for the next five KPIs. Standardization is a loop, not a launch event.

Leaders should ask one question: can every add-on defend the same margin sentence, or are we still translating plant dialects in the room? If you are still translating, you have dashboards, not acquisition performance visibility.

## Why visuals first feels faster, and isn't

Charting existing plant extracts looks like progress in week one, and applause is cheap.

In week four, someone asks for cross-plant customer margin. The extracts do not share customer keys and the demo stalls. You rebuild anyway, and now you also have to unteach the temporary tiles.

Definition-first feels slower because the arguments happen up front. That is the point. An argument in a working session costs less than the same argument in a board meeting.

Power BI features help when they enforce a governed semantic model, explicit measures, shared grain, and clear ownership. They do not write the business sentence. People do.

## Executive takeaway

PE wants the same margin and working-capital language across add-ons, and five plant Excels will fail the first board pack. Name grain and measures before visuals, own the dictionary, and make the semantic model the product the portfolio runs on.
