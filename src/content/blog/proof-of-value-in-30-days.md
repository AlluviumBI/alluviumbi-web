Thirty days should change one meeting. A 40-page roadmap does not.

A mid-market team that spends the month on a future estate has already failed the only test that matters this cycle: did one decision start using one trusted number? One loop with one owner is proof. Everything else is a sequel.

![Black-and-white seedling with first leaves in dark soil](/blog/proof-of-value-in-30-days-hero.jpg)

## This is a proof, not a program

[The first 90 days of a Power BI program](/blog/first-90-days-of-a-power-bi-program) covers the operating work: one model, one app, and one owner for a quarter, then copy the method. That calendar is not repeated here.

This piece is about the gate *before* you fund a program. A proof of value runs four weeks on one decision loop. If the meeting does not move, you do not get a lake, a CoE, or a phase-two slide. You get an honest stop.

Leaders search “Power BI proof of concept” after a vendor workshop that felt expensive. They were shown a catalog when they needed a Tuesday that changed.

A proof is not a sample dataset and a pretty page. Sample data trains the room to ignore the next link. A proof uses the dirt you already have, on the grain finance or ops will actually argue about, in a meeting that already exists.

If day thirty still points at a roadmap, you ran a workshop. You did not prove value.

## What a slide-first thirty days costs

1. **The meeting does not notice.** You designed a future while the pack is still email. Nobody can say the number moved. The month was theater, and the P&L did not care.

2. **Scope inflates to look strategic.** Without a cut, “proof” becomes three domains, twelve KPIs, and a platform diagram. Busy is not evidence. Intake without a no is how you [commission a dashboard per question](/blog/stop-commissioning-a-dashboard-per-question).

3. **Steering hears architecture.** Green on “model in progress” is not control. Control means this measure is signed, this pack was used, and this twin stayed closed for one cycle. If Monday still starts with a dump, the status was decoration.

4. **You buy the sequel before the pilot.** Capacity, extra SKUs, and another tool get bought because the demo implied a stack. [Don’t buy another tool until one model works](/blog/dont-buy-another-tool-until-one-model-works). A proof that requires a new platform is not a proof. It is a purchase order.

5. **Trust never gets a chance.** A polished fake trains skepticism, and the next thirty days start underwater. Dirty real data that leadership will sign beats a clean fiction.

6. **The program inherits a lie.** Roadmaps funded on a workshop become a year of status reports. Delivery was the real risk all along, because [analytics programs fail in delivery](/blog/analytics-programs-fail-in-delivery). Proof is how you refuse to start that year.

You do not need a promised ROI percentage. If the chair still asks for the file, the proof failed.

## What not to do in thirty days

Do not inventory the company. Catalogs without a live pack become another report.

Do not staff a center of excellence. A CoE without a ship date is a committee.

Do not measure the month by report count. Measure it by one signed measure in one meeting, with the competing extract unused for that decision.

Do not wait for a pristine lake. Pick a grain the consumer will sign, using the sources that exist. Waiting for perfect is how day thirty ends up empty.

Do not call a sandbox “production” to hit the date. A proof that cannot refresh on a clock the meeting can live with is a screenshot.

## The thirty-day loop: one decision, one number, one meeting

1. **Days 1 to 5: pick the meeting and the question.** Decide which Tuesday, which number, who sits in the chair, and who may freeze the definition. Write it down. If two questions are “equally urgent,” pick the one that already burns a hero every week, as in [questions that should never be ad-hoc](/blog/questions-that-should-never-be-adhoc). Strategy without a named decision is just more dashboards.

2. **Days 3 to 12: freeze the grain and a short measure list.** Skip the forty KPIs. Write one to five sentences covering include, exclude, grain, owner, and as-of, because [unexplained measures are not KPIs](/blog/measures-nobody-can-explain). Connect sources that already exist. Do not rebuild the ERP or open a second domain.

3. **Days 8 to 18: stand up the smallest model that can tell the truth.** Build relationships that match how you ship or book, refresh on a clock the meeting can live with, and access that matches who may see each slice. If the meeting is finance, a failed morning job is already [close risk](/blog/refresh-failures-are-a-close-risk). Put refresh on the same board as the build.

4. **Days 12 to 22: put one pack in the real meeting.** Ugly is allowed. Live is not optional. Point the agenda at it and name the twin you will not use for this decision. If Excel is still right for commentary, connect it instead of pasting, as covered in [Excel vs Power BI](/blog/excel-vs-power-bi-financial-reporting). Sit in the room and capture every “that’s not my number.” That list is the only backlog.

5. **Days 18 to 28: fix definitions, not pages.** Treat refusal as a spec. Finance sign-off is a feature, as [why finance won’t sign the dashboard](/blog/finance-wont-sign-off-on-the-dashboard) explains. Do not add visuals to dodge the argument. Run the meeting a second time on the same product.

6. **Days 25 to 30: write the proof, not the museum.** Did the meeting use it? Did the twin stay closed for that question? Is there a named owner? What would the *next* thirty days copy? If the answer is “we need a roadmap first,” you learned the truth, so do not fund a program to hide it. If the answer is yes, the quarter can start, and the 90-day article is earned.

A [Quickstart](/power-bi-quickstart) has this shape: one automated loop, not a brochure factory. Change work belongs in week one, through [data project management and change leadership](/data-project-management-change-leadership). If nobody can name the decision, a [roadmap](/analytics-ai-strategy-roadmap) is the proof you actually need, written on paper in days, not a 40-page stall.

## What good looks like on day thirty

The chair can say where the number lives without a hunt, and the steward can say what the measure includes in one sentence.

The meeting opened the product at least twice, and the old file for that question stayed shut. The next ask lands in a backlog, not a side deal.

That is enough to fund a program. A lakehouse can wait. A second department can wait. A slide of future dashboards can wait forever.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is thirty days enough to certify a domain?**
It is enough to prove one decision loop. Certifying a whole domain takes the quarter. Do not pretend a proof is a program.

**What if source data is dirty?**
Use it and name the dirt. A signed dirty number beats an unsigned clean demo. See [data quality shows up as arguments](/blog/data-quality-shows-up-as-arguments).

**Can we prove two meetings in parallel?**
Usually not. Two meetings mean two owners and two grains, and that is how both stay half-done. Sequence them.
