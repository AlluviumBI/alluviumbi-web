Go-live is not the win. The first exec meeting after is when trust is decided.

The launch email went out. The app is in the workspace. None of that is the test. The test is Tuesday: who is in the room, which file they open, and whether the first hard question gets an answer or a follow-up.

![Black-and-white empty dirt road after rain, puddles reflecting sky](/blog/the-meeting-after-go-live-hero.jpg)

## This is not “nobody opens the dashboard”

Unused reports over weeks—wrong grain, slow pages, no owner—are an operating problem: [nobody opens the dashboard](/blog/nobody-opens-the-dashboard). That article is the empty usage log. This post is the first hour after you claimed victory.

Change theater—training that does not retire the twin—is [change management for analytics](/blog/change-management-for-analytics). Do not retread posters here. Hypercare is the named window after go-live: who sits in the first meeting, who answers the first challenge, what happens when the number and the controller disagree in public.

A program with no owner never reaches a clean first meeting: [why your Power BI project has no owner](/blog/power-bi-project-has-no-owner). The first ninety days are a loop, not a portal: [the first 90 days of a Power BI program](/blog/first-90-days-of-a-power-bi-program). Here the unit is one meeting. One pack. One chance to keep or lose the room.

[Judgment:] if the steward is not in the first exec meeting, you did not go live. You published.

## What the first meeting actually tests

It tests whether the page is the job. If the agenda still runs from last month’s workbook “just this once,” the twin stays.

It tests the sentence when a VP pushes, the as-of stamp versus the controller’s file, and who is allowed to speak. IT should not narrate Revenue. Finance should not hear the definition for the first time. Steward, report owner, consumer leader: if any of those three is “busy this week,” reschedule. Do not send the email anyway.

Sign-off before the sitting is necessary: [why finance won’t sign off on the dashboard](/blog/finance-wont-sign-off-on-the-dashboard). The meeting is still where trust is spent.

## The costs of treating go-live as the finish

1. **The first mismatch becomes the story.** One unexplained gap versus the controller and the app is “the thing we don’t use.” You will spend months undoing a ten-minute silence. Trust is not a color palette. It is what happens when the number is challenged.

2. **Hypercare becomes inbox chaos.** Without a named window and a named responder, every ping hits the person who built the page. They are in another kickoff. The business learns the product has no adult in the room.

3. **The old pack stays “for backup.”** Backup is how twins become permanent. After the first meeting still used the workbook, you have two official truths. Change leadership that does not retire is a duplicate cost.

4. **Leaders discount the next launch.** After a go-live that folded in the first ELT, the plant will sit through the next demo and change nothing. Program trust is a finite stock. You spent it on a publish event.

5. **The backlog inherits fiction.** Roadmaps add pages on top of a kernel the room already abandoned. You fund a museum. Usage will look fine to analysts. The meeting will still run on Excel.

6. **The steward is never socialized.** If the first meeting is a tool tour by the implementer, the business never meets the person who owns the sentence. Later questions go to whoever is convenient. The model becomes a wiki.

You do not need an attendance metric from a vendor blog. Watch the agenda. If the new page was not the working file, go-live failed—even if the workspace says Published.

## Who sits in the room

**Consumer leader.** The person whose meeting this is. They open the app. They take the old slide out of the pack. If they will not, stop. Training will not override a chair who still wants the workbook.

**Steward.** The person who will defend the measure. Not “the analyst who published.” When the VP asks what Revenue includes, this mouth moves.

**Modeler or report owner for hypercare.** They fix grain, filters, and broken refresh in hours, not in a sprint two weeks later. They are on the invite. They are on the bridge.

A kickoff crowd is not a decision room. Platform IT can be on call. They should not narrate the number.

## How to win the meeting after go-live

1. **Schedule hypercare as part of delivery.** Name the window: the first two or three meetings that use the page. Named responder. Named backup. After-hours path if this is a close week. If hypercare is “we’ll watch the tickets,” you already staffed nobody.

2. **Pre-fight the first questions.** Write the five challenges you know are coming: timing, exclusions, plant cuts, why it differs from last month’s file. Answer them in a one-page sentence sheet the steward brings. Do not discover the definition in front of the CEO.

3. **Match the stamp to the meeting.** Refresh finished. As-of visible. If the controller’s pack is same-day and the model is T-1, say so before anyone asks—or fix the clock. A surprise lag is how you lose Tuesday. Refresh as close risk is [refresh failures](/blog/refresh-failures-are-a-close-risk).

4. **Open the app, retire the slide.** The consumer leader does it. Not the vendor. Not IT. The old file is out of the deck for this meeting, not “available if we need it.” If both remain, the old one wins. That stop is change management. This is the first time you actually do it.

5. **Treat the first mismatch as a product incident.** Same day. Steward plus modeler. Either the sentence was wrong, the grain was wrong, or the source was late. Say which. Patch. Do not shrug and say “we’ll look.” A shrug in the first meeting is a verdict.

6. **Debrief once, in writing.** What question the page could not take. What will change before the next sitting. What will not change because the sentence is the sentence. Then the window ends. Endless hypercare is how a project never has an owner.

## What good looks like

The chair opens the app. The pack and the screen match. Someone drills instead of promising a file.

The steward answers a challenge in a sentence. A defect is fixed before the next sitting, or declined with a reason. The workbook is not on the table. Hypercare ends on a date.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can we go live without finance in the first meeting?**
You can publish. You cannot earn trust. If finance is the consumer, they chair. If they are a stakeholder, they still have to see the sentence before ELT does.

**What if the first meeting is next month?**
Then go-live is next month. Do not burn the word on a quiet publish. Trust is spent in the room that matters.

**Isn’t this just training?**
No. Training is click paths. This is who speaks, which file dies, and how a challenge is closed the same day.
