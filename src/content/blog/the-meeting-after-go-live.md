Go-live is not the win. Trust is decided in the first executive meeting afterward.

The launch email went out and the app is in the workspace. None of that is the test. The test is Tuesday: who is in the room, which file they open, and whether the first hard question gets an answer or a follow-up.

![Black-and-white empty dirt road after rain, puddles reflecting sky](/blog/the-meeting-after-go-live-hero.jpg)

## This is not “nobody opens the dashboard”

Reports that sit unused for weeks because of wrong grain, slow pages, or no owner are an operating problem, covered in [nobody opens the dashboard](/blog/nobody-opens-the-dashboard). That article is about the empty usage log. This post is about the first hour after you claimed victory.

Change theater, meaning training that never retires the twin, is covered in [change management for analytics](/blog/change-management-for-analytics). Hypercare is the named window after go-live: who sits in the first meeting, who answers the first challenge, and what happens when the number and the controller disagree in public.

A program with no owner never reaches a clean first meeting, as [why your Power BI project has no owner](/blog/power-bi-project-has-no-owner) explains. [The first 90 days of a Power BI program](/blog/first-90-days-of-a-power-bi-program) are a loop, not a portal. Here the unit is one meeting, one pack, and one chance to keep or lose the room.

If the steward is not in the first executive meeting, you did not go live. You published.

## What the first meeting actually tests

It tests whether the page is the job. If the agenda still runs from last month’s workbook “just this once,” the twin stays.

It also tests the sentence when a VP pushes, the as-of stamp against the controller’s file, and who gets to speak. IT should not narrate Revenue, and finance should not hear the definition for the first time. The steward, the report owner, and the consumer leader all need to be there. If any of them is “busy this week,” reschedule instead of sending the email anyway.

Sign-off before the meeting is necessary, as covered in [why finance won’t sign off on the dashboard](/blog/finance-wont-sign-off-on-the-dashboard). The meeting is still where trust gets spent.

## The costs of treating go-live as the finish

1. **The first mismatch becomes the story.** One unexplained gap against the controller and the app becomes “the thing we don’t use.” You will spend months undoing a ten-minute silence. Trust is not a color palette. It is what happens when the number is challenged.

2. **Hypercare becomes inbox chaos.** Without a named window and a named responder, every ping hits the person who built the page, who is already in another kickoff. The business learns there is no adult in the room.

3. **The old pack stays “for backup.”** Backup is how twins become permanent. If the first meeting still used the workbook, you now have two official truths. Change leadership that does not retire anything is a duplicate cost.

4. **Leaders discount the next launch.** After a go-live that folded in the first ELT, the plant will sit through the next demo and change nothing. Program trust is a finite stock, and you spent it on a publish event.

5. **The backlog inherits fiction.** Roadmaps add pages on top of a model the room already abandoned, and you fund a museum. Usage looks fine to analysts while the meeting still runs on Excel.

6. **The business never meets the steward.** If the first meeting is a tool tour by the implementer, nobody meets the person who owns the sentence. Later questions go to whoever is convenient, and the model becomes a wiki.

You do not need an attendance metric from a vendor blog. Watch the agenda. If the new page was not the working file, go-live failed, even if the workspace says Published.

## Who sits in the room

**Consumer leader.** This is the person whose meeting it is. They open the app and take the old slide out of the pack. If they will not, stop. Training will not override a chair who still wants the workbook.

**Steward.** This is the person who will defend the measure, not “the analyst who published.” When the VP asks what Revenue includes, the steward answers.

**Modeler or report owner for hypercare.** They fix grain, filters, and broken refresh in hours, not in a sprint two weeks later. They are on the invite and on the bridge.

A kickoff crowd is not a decision room. Platform IT can be on call, but they should not narrate the number.

## How to win the meeting after go-live

1. **Schedule hypercare as part of delivery.** Name the window as the first two or three meetings that use the page, with a named responder, a named backup, and an after-hours path if it is close week. If hypercare means “we’ll watch the tickets,” you have staffed nobody.

2. **Rehearse the first questions.** Write down the five challenges you know are coming: timing, exclusions, plant cuts, and why the number differs from last month’s file. Answer them on a one-page sheet the steward brings. Do not discover the definition in front of the CEO.

3. **Match the stamp to the meeting.** Refresh is finished and the as-of is visible. If the controller’s pack is same-day and the model is T-1, say so before anyone asks, or fix the clock. A surprise lag is how you lose Tuesday. Refresh as close risk is covered in [refresh failures](/blog/refresh-failures-are-a-close-risk).

4. **Open the app and retire the slide.** The consumer leader does this, not the vendor and not IT. The old file is out of the deck for this meeting, not “available if we need it.” If both remain, the old one wins. This is the moment change management actually happens.

5. **Treat the first mismatch as a product incident.** The steward and the modeler take it the same day. Either the sentence was wrong, the grain was wrong, or the source was late. Say which and patch it. A shrug and a “we’ll look” in the first meeting is a verdict.

6. **Debrief once, in writing.** Note which question the page could not take, what will change before the next meeting, and what will not change because the sentence is the sentence. Then end the window. Endless hypercare is how a project never gets an owner.

## What good looks like

The chair opens the app, the pack and the screen match, and someone drills down instead of promising a file. The steward answers a challenge in one sentence. Defects are fixed before the next meeting or declined with a reason. The workbook is not on the table, and hypercare ends on a date.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can we go live without finance in the first meeting?**
You can publish, but you cannot earn trust. If finance is the consumer, finance chairs. If finance is a stakeholder, it still has to see the sentence before the ELT does.

**What if the first meeting is next month?**
Then go-live is next month. Do not burn the word on a quiet publish. Trust is spent in the room that matters.

**Isn’t this just training?**
No. Training covers click paths. This covers who speaks, which file dies, and how a challenge gets closed the same day.
