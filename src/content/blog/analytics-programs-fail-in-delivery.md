The stack is rarely why a Power BI program stalls. Scope, owners, and a backlog are.

Mid-market companies do not fail because they picked the wrong visual. They fail because nobody runs the work like work, with a named outcome, a cut line, a cadence, and a person who can say no.

![Black-and-white unfinished dry-stone wall with a gap in a field](/blog/analytics-programs-fail-in-delivery-hero.jpg)

## The tool did not stall. The program did.

Power BI will render whatever you feed it. It will also host a two-year “phase two” that never ships a decision.

The stall has a familiar shape. There is a kickoff with a strategy slide and a proof that impresses. Then comes a waiting list of dashboards, no product owner, and a steering committee that meets to hear that “IT is still sourcing.” Six months later the ELT still asks for the number in email. That is not a licensing problem. It is delivery.

Strategy still matters. If you cannot name the decisions, you will deliver inventory. This piece is about what happens after the strategy slide: PMO, scope, and change. That is the work Alluvium scopes as [data project management and change leadership](https://www.alluviumbi.com/data-project-management-change-leadership).

A [platform bake-off](/blog/fabric-vs-power-bi-for-a-ceo) is often the stall in costume. So is [waiting on IT for every cut](/blog/hidden-cost-waiting-on-it-for-reports). Both are delivery failures dressed up as architecture or tickets.

## What “delivery” means for analytics

Delivery is not a Gantt chart that lists every report. It is a backlog of decisions and data products. Each item has a requester, a steward, a definition of done, and a date. Anything missing those four is a conversation, not work.

Delivery needs an owner who can cut scope. If the only move is to add another dashboard, you do not have a program. You have a queue.

It needs a cadence that surfaces risk while the close is still this month. Refresh, access, and sign-off belong on the same board as “build the page.”

And it includes change work from week one: who will stop using the old file, who trains the plant controller, and what happens on the Monday after go-live. Delivery that ignores adoption ships a museum.

## The costs of tool-first programs

1. **Scope becomes a museum of requests.** Every VP adds a page and nobody ranks them. The team is busy, but nothing that would change Tuesday gets done. Busy is not a KPI.

2. **Nobody can say no, so nobody can finish.** Without a cut line, “done” is a feeling and the project never ends. It is a close cousin of [a Power BI project with no owner](/blog/power-bi-project-has-no-owner). Delivery needs a name on the yes and the no.

3. **Steering hears status, not decisions.** Green, yellow, and red on “model in progress” is not control. Control is knowing which metric was certified this month, which report was retired, and which plant is live. If the pack still arrives late, the status color was decoration.

4. **Change gets scheduled after go-live.** Users see a new link and keep the spreadsheet. You declared victory on a deploy, but the operating cadence did not move. From the seat count, that looks like [unused Power BI licenses](/blog/unused-power-bi-licenses) and empty dashboards.

5. **Vendors and internal teams run in parallel without one backlog.** IT has a list, finance has a list, and a partner has a list. Three lists are how you rebuild the same grain three times and still miss the board pack.

6. **The next tool looks like the fix.** When delivery is messy, a new platform feels clean. So you migrate the mess. You did not install a PMO. You installed a delay.

## What not to do

Do not start with a six-month architecture freeze. Prove one product in a quarter, then expand the landing zone if you must.

Do not staff a “center of excellence” as a substitute for a backlog. A CoE without intake and retire rules is another committee.

Do not measure delivery by report count. Measure certified measures in use, cycle time to a named decision, and files actually retired.

Do not treat change as a training deck the week before launch. Operators need to know what they will stop doing, and leaders need to stop asking for the old file.

## How to fix delivery

1. **Name one program owner who can cut.** A person, not a committee, who can rank the backlog against the P&L. IT can run the platform and finance can own definitions, but someone has to own the sequence. Put that name in writing.

2. **Build a backlog of products, not pages.** “Certified margin model, plant grain, signed by controller, live in the Monday pack” is an item. “Sales dashboard v3” is not. Tie each item to a decision, and if you cannot, keep it out of the sprint.

3. **Time-box a first release leadership will actually use.** Ninety days, one domain, one pack, and one retire list for the twins. [Proof of value](/blog/proof-of-value-in-30-days) gets its own post. The rule here is simpler: if the first release cannot change a meeting, it is a demo.

4. **Put refresh, access, and sign-off on the same board as build.** A page that cannot refresh is not done. A model without [row-level security](/blog/row-level-security-who-sees-the-number), where plants must not see each other, is not done. A measure finance will not sign is not done. Done includes operations.

5. **Run change inside the sprint.** Decide who loses the old workbook, who gets trained, and who answers the first week of “where did my tab go.” Assign it and budget it. If adoption is “later,” later never comes. When you do not have the bench, hands-on delivery and change is what our [data project management service](https://www.alluviumbi.com/data-project-management-change-leadership) is for.

6. **Keep one intake path.** All requests hit the same backlog, because shadow projects are how sprawl returns. If a VP can commission a side file, you do not have a program. You have a suggestion box with licenses.

Start small with one department and one high-value product, then iterate. It is the same discipline as governance: prove it, then scale. It is not a reason to wait for a perfect PMO handbook.

## What good looks like

The ELT can point to a ranked list. This month’s ship is named, and so is next month’s cut.

Status reports outcomes (certified, retired, adopted) instead of hours burned.

Go-live has a meeting after it. People show up with the new pack, the old file is gone, and questions go to the steward instead of a Slack archaeology hunt.

When something slips, the owner cuts scope. They do not add a tool.

## FAQ

**Is this a waterfall vs agile argument?** No. Use whatever cadence your company already runs. The constants are a backlog, a cut, and visible risk. Methodology theater is just another stall.

**Can IT “just deliver Power BI” without a PMO?** IT can host it. A program that touches close, plants, and commercial still needs a business owner and a single intake. Hosting is not delivery.

**Do we need a partner to run this?** Only if nobody in-house can own sequence, stakeholders, and change at the same time. Many mid-market teams borrow a lead for two quarters, then run it themselves.
